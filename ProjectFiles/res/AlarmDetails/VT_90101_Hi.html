Alarm Response Plan

Tag: VT-90101 — Compressor Motor Vibration Hi (Drive End)

1. Alarm Summary

Alarm TagVT-90101.Hi
MeasurementMotor drive end bearing vibration (RMS velocity)
Alarm DescriptionVibration exceeded the Hi warning setpoint — pre-alarm before HiHi trip
Hi Setpoint (Example)> 4.5 mm/s RMS
PriorityMEDIUM
Alarm ClassProcess / Early Warning
Response Time< 30 minutes
Consequence of InactionEscalation to Hi-Hi trip, drive-end bearing degradation, coupling damage

2. Possible Causes

  • Coupling misalignment or wear (drive end is most sensitive to coupling faults).
  • Developing bearing defect at the drive end.
  • Loose motor mounting or soft foot.
  • Rotor imbalance / rub.
  • Process-induced vibration transmitted through the shaft.
  • Sensor / cabling fault (verify with second sensor).
Note: Drive-end vibration alarms are frequently caused by coupling and alignment issues. Compare with VT-90102 (non-drive end) — a drive-end-only signature strongly implicates the coupling.

3. Operator Response Actions

  1. Acknowledge the alarm and review VT-90101 trend for last 24 h.
  2. Compare VT-90101 with VT-90102 — is elevation isolated to the drive end?
  3. Field walk-down — inspect coupling guard, listen for rubbing, check for oil/grease throw.
  4. Notify Reliability / Vibration Analyst to collect spectra.
  5. If vibration is climbing toward HiHi, plan an orderly shutdown before the trip.

4. Troubleshooting Guidance

  1. Capture time-waveform and FFT at VT-90101 and adjacent bearings.
  2. Check bearing temperature trend if instrumented.
  3. Inspect coupling for wear, missing bolts, or elastomer damage at next opportunity.
  4. Verify alignment records — consider laser alignment check.
  5. Confirm sensor and cable integrity if reading is uncharacteristic.

5. Return to Normal

  • Vibration returns below Hi setpoint minus deadband.
  • Analyst review indicates no imminent failure, or a work order is generated for repair.
  • Alarm clears automatically once reading normalizes.
