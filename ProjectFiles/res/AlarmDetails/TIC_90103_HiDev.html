Alarm Response Plan

Tag: TIC-90103 — Compressor Oil Temperature PID Hi Deviation from SP

1. Alarm Summary

Alarm TagTIC-90103.HiDev
LoopCompressor oil temperature control loop (PV: TT-90103, MV: TCV-90103)
Alarm DescriptionProcess variable is above setpoint by more than the configured deviation threshold for the required time
Deviation Threshold (Example)PV − SP > 5 °C for > 60 s
PriorityMEDIUM
Alarm ClassProcess Control
Response Time< 15 minutes
Consequence of InactionEscalation to TT-90103 Hi and eventually HiHi trip

2. Possible Causes

  • Loop in Manual with cooling water valve throttled.
  • TCV-90103 stuck or failing to stroke to output demand.
  • PID output saturated at 100% and still not enough cooling.
  • Loss of cooling water supply.
  • Controller de-tuned (integral windup, wrong PID gains).
  • Setpoint changed to a value that exceeds control capability.
  • PV signal problem (see TT_90103_Fail plan).

3. Operator Response Actions

  1. Acknowledge the alarm.
  2. Open the TIC-90103 faceplate — verify Mode (Auto vs Manual), SP, PV, OUT, and setpoint tracking.
  3. If in Manual, return to Auto if safe; otherwise increase cooling water manually.
  4. If OUT is at 100% (open), field-verify TCV-90103 is actually open and cooling water is flowing.
  5. Check cooling water header conditions.
  6. If PV continues to climb, refer to TT_90103_Hi plan and prepare for potential trip response.

4. Troubleshooting Guidance

  1. Bump-test TCV-90103 in Manual (small step) to confirm valve stroke.
  2. Check positioner air supply and I/P feedback.
  3. Review controller tuning history and last tuning date.
  4. Verify PID direct/reverse action and valve fail action are correct.
  5. Compare PV against a bearing RTD to rule out sensor drift.
Note: A persistent Hi deviation with OUT saturated typically indicates a process/equipment issue (valve, cooling supply), not a controller tuning issue.

5. Return to Normal

  • PV returns within the deviation deadband of SP.
  • Loop stable in Auto for at least 5 minutes.
  • Root cause corrected and documented.
