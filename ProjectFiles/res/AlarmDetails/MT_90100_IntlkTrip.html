Alarm Response Plan

Tag: MT-90100 — Compressor Motor Interlock Trip

1. Alarm Summary

Alarm TagMT-90100.IntlkTrip
EquipmentCompressor Motor MT-90100
Alarm DescriptionMotor interlock trip — motor stopped by protective interlock
PriorityHIGH / CRITICAL
Alarm ClassSafety / Equipment Protection
Response TimeImmediate (< 5 minutes)
Consequence of InactionLoss of process availability, potential equipment damage, downstream upset

2. Possible Causes

MT-90100 will trip on any of the following interlock conditions:

Interlock Trip Setpoint (Example) Typical Cause
High Oil Temperature > 85 °C Loss of lube oil cooling, low oil flow, fouled cooler, ambient heat load, degraded oil
High Motor Current > 110% FLA for > 5 s Process overload, discharge blockage, bearing failure, coupling misalignment, single-phasing
High Vibration > 7.1 mm/s RMS Imbalance, misalignment, bearing wear, loose mounting, surge event
Safety Notice: Do NOT attempt to restart MT-90100 until the tripping interlock has been identified, the root cause corrected, and the trip signal reset per site LOTO and startup procedures.

3. Operator Response Actions

  1. Acknowledge the alarm on the operator station and record the time of trip.
  2. Confirm the motor has stopped and the discharge check valve has seated. Verify no reverse rotation.
  3. Identify which interlock initiated the trip via the MT-90100 faceplate – First Out indication.
  4. Place downstream users of MT-90100 in a safe state (open bypass, load-shed, or switch to spare unit MT-90101 if available).
  5. Notify the Shift Supervisor and log the event in the shift log and CMMS.
  6. Follow the branch below that matches the First Out interlock.

4. First-Out Specific Actions

4.1 High Oil Temperature Trip

  1. Check lube oil cooler cooling water supply – verify flow, temperature, and valve position.
  2. Check lube oil pump discharge pressure and reservoir level.
  3. Inspect oil cooler for fouling or air-side blockage (if air-cooled).
  4. Sample oil for degradation / contamination if temperature history shows a trend.
  5. Do not reset until oil temperature is < 60 °C and root cause is confirmed.

4.2 High Motor Current Trip

  1. Review last 15 minutes of trends: motor current, suction/discharge pressure, flow, and speed.
  2. Check for closed or restricted discharge valve, plugged strainer, or process upset upstream.
  3. Request electrical technician verify motor windings (megger) and check for single-phase condition.
  4. Verify coupling and driven-end bearing condition – rotate by hand if safe.
  5. Do not attempt more than one restart. Repeated overcurrent trips require a mechanical inspection.

4.3 High Vibration Trip

  1. Review vibration trend and identify which probe/axis exceeded the trip level.
  2. Visually inspect motor and driven equipment for loose fasteners, oil leaks, or coupling damage.
  3. Check baseplate and grouting for looseness.
  4. Request vibration analyst to pull spectra before restart to identify imbalance / misalignment / bearing defect.
  5. Do NOT restart if vibration cause is unconfirmed – risk of catastrophic bearing failure.
Note: If more than one interlock is active, treat as a compound event. Escalate to Maintenance and Reliability Engineering before any restart attempt.

5. Reset and Restart Criteria

  • Root cause identified and documented.
  • All interlock inputs healthy and below alarm setpoints.
  • Field walk-down completed – no leaks, no abnormal sounds, guarding in place.
  • Shift Supervisor approval logged.
  • Reset MT-90100 interlock latch at faceplate, then follow standard start sequence (raP_Opr_Seq step: Pre-Start → Start → Running).
